# Design the team for a consequential decomposition

Start from the outcome, authority, current artifacts, uncertainty, dependencies,
available capabilities and cost constraints. Reuse adequate analysis. Choose the
smallest process that can deliver the agreed result; a familiar bounded repair needs
only a coder and an independent reviewer under its existing owner.

Apply the context rule from your guidance. Put each job where the context it needs
already is, and do not route context through a head that does not need it. Group
functions that share substantial evolving plans, evidence and decisions when their
authority is compatible. A lane, work with its own producer and reviewer or its own
landing sequence, normally gets its own orchestrator, which opens the lane's assignments
and settles its workers' traffic and may redesign that subtree. That owner is not a
forwarding layer, because it decides on what it receives; avoid a layer that only
relays. Keep cross-subtree contracts with an identified delivery owner and arrange
direct specialist exchange where useful.

Account for what existing agents already know. Reuse a suitable agent that can take
relevant changes without repeated catch-up; delegate when the needed context travels
economically. Give each child its outcome, authority and relevant evidence, and leave
unrelated history out. Use preferred-models for frugal selection by expected total
cost, including supervision, repeated context, corrective work, idle roles and
possible cache reuse. Use the planning activity in preferred-models and scale
reasoning to the decomposition's length and complexity.

Compose the elected archetypes first: orchestrator, product-owner, spec-writer, coder,
reviewer-code, reviewer-spec, recon, guidance-writer, guidance-reviewer and integrator.
Vary their number, scope, model, effort and lifetime. A new archetype needs a distinct
reusable responsibility, not merely a task name; describe its authority, needed
context and expected output for guidance-writer authoring and independent guidance
review. An invented name supplies neither capabilities nor runtime mechanisms.

Carry an addressed product role and spirit reference into each brief. Keep the PO
available for proactive product judgment, new specifications and material intent
questions. Plan useful lifetime: which help ends with its result, which context merits
reuse and who retires each agent, under the operating manual's retention and teardown
duty. Decide no new agent when the current team is adequate.

Reassess on changed coupling, repeated clarification, corrective supervision, cost or
unowned work. Preserve usable work. Role rebinding and new spawns do not reparent
existing sessions or transfer open assignments; inspect actual custody and recovery
routes before treating a topology repair as complete. Main remains the substrate's
last-resort address, not a routine worker. Keep topology a judgment made for the job;
introduce no fixed agent graph, mandatory stage count or model-provider gate.
