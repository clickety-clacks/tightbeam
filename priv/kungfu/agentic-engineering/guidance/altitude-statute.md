# The altitude statute (template)

A statute template an org arms by ordinary law. Its one sentence: the product owner
judges; the orchestrator coordinates; neither works the other's altitude.

    STATUTE — altitude
    1. Product owners do not assign implementation cards directly.
    2. Every product work item carries an orchestrator, and that orchestrator
       owns the LIFETIME of its work items and their toplines — staffing,
       sequencing, review commissioning, dispositions — under the product
       owner's spirit rulings.
    3. A PO who finds a card only an implementer should hold routes it through
       the work item's orchestrator; no orchestrator, hire one first (the
       office is the unit of hiring).

Why a statute and not a habit: the failure it prevents is quiet and comfortable —
an owner staffing coders and running reviews personally while orchestrators sit
idle looks like diligence and is actually the org's judgment layer doing its
coordination layer's job, badly, at frontier prices. Evidence of need is on the
record from live operation; the statute makes the altitude a rule someone can cite
when it drifts, instead of a preference someone must argue.

The statute binds altitude, never judgment: the PO's spirit rulings still govern
everything the orchestrator does, and acceptance still belongs to the PO. Arming,
amending, or repealing this statute is an ordinary org law action, on the record.
