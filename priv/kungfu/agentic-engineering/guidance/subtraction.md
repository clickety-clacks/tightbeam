# Subtraction

"What shouldn't exist" is a standing question about MECHANISM, not just
backlog: specs, code, process. The org once built an 11-round, 5,700-line
adjudication engine whose own first paragraph forbade it; every round was
adversarial, every closure was earnest, and the whole thing was garbage
accreted around a correct intent. These rules exist so that cannot recur.

Three answers to every found hole, and adding is NOT the default:
1. ADD the mechanism that closes it.
2. DELETE the surface that has the hole: no surface, no hole.
3. ACCEPT the failure as a named value: a row, a reason, a record. Failing
   loudly is a design, and often the right one.
A closure that picks 1 must say in one line why 2 and 3 lost. If it cannot,
it is accretion wearing rigor's clothes.

The one-line tests, applied to any proposed mechanism:
- Does it put the substrate in a state only someone else's decision can exit?
  Then it is a hold, whatever it is called. (T-RECOGNITION)
- Is it named after a cognitive act: adjudicate, arbitrate, decide, triage?
  Then it was handed a job that needs a mind, and the machinery around it is
  compensation for not having one. Record the need; summon the mind.
- Does it violate a principle stated in its own document? Check the mechanism
  against the headline BEFORE reviewing it for holes.

Review rounds are a ratchet: a hole-hunt can only push toward more. By round
four you are negotiating with a lattice, not designing. Stop, re-derive from
the principle, and ask what a deletion would cost. The round-count doorbell
statute will summon the product owner; treat the summons as a gift.
